### Directions
Solve the following problems and write up your solutions.  Your solutions should be provided in one of the following formats (in order of preference)
* typed up in $$\LaTeX$$ and submitted as a PDF on Canvas
* written legibly on blank paper, scanned into a PDF and then uploaded on Canvas
* written on ancient parchement with a quill and then flown to the instructor via owl post like in Harry Potter

If you go with the first strategy, you may wish to check out Overleaf which is a free and intuitive website for generating $$\LaTeX$$ documents online.
If you wish to use the second method and don't own a scanner at home, you can check out the numerous scanning apps available for smartphones.

You will be graded based on *completion* of all of the assigned problems, along with in-depth grading of *select* problems which will not be revealed until after the homework is graded.

**Remember:** Success in any math class is based on *practice*.  The assigned homework problems are the **bare minimum**.  You should strive to do as many problems as possible from the textbook.

**Note:** All sets will be subsets of $$\mathbb R$$ unless otherwise stated.

### Problems

**Problem 1:** 
Carefully prove each of the following limits (with the Euclidean metric)

a) $$\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} 2^{-n} =  0$$

b) $$\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} \frac{2^{-n}}{n!} =  0$$

c) $$\lim_{x\rightarrow 2} x^2 = 4.$$

d) $$\lim_{x\rightarrow 1} \frac{\sqrt{x^2+3}-2}{x-1} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

**Problem 2:**

Let $$(M,d)$$ be a metric space and suppose $$A\subseteq M$$ is closed and $$K\subseteq M$$ is compact.
Prove that $$A\cap K$$ is also compact.

**Problem 3:**

Consider the function $$f: \mathbb R^2\rightarrow\mathbb R$$ defined by $$f(0,0) = 2025$$ and

$$f(x,y) = \frac{x^2-y^2}{x^2+y^2},\quad (x,y)\neq (0,0).$$

Evaluate each of the following limits or show that they do not exist.

a) $$\lim_{(x,y)\rightarrow (1,0)} f(x,y)$$

b) $$\lim_{(x,y)\rightarrow (0,1)} f(x,y)$$

c) $$\lim_{(x,y)\rightarrow (0,0)} f(x,y)$$


**Problem 4:**

Prove that if $$\{x_n\}$$ is a Cauchy sequence and the set

$$X = \{x_1,x_2,x_3,\dots\}$$

is finite, then $$x_n$$ converges.
